What it is
The council coordinating the 22 national bishops' conferences of Latin America and the Caribbean — per its statutes an organ of 'contact, communion and service' among conferences: coordinative, not a magisterial body.[1] [15]
Founded
1955 — petitioned by the I General Conference in Rio de Janeiro (25 July – 4 August, convoked by Pius XII) and approved by the pope that autumn, formally on 2 November 1955; sources differ on the exact approval sequence. ?[1] [16] [15]
Headquarters
Bogotá, Colombia — the General Secretariat has functioned there since the founding years.[15] [6]
The five General Conferences
Rio de Janeiro 1955, Medellín 1968, Puebla 1979, Santo Domingo 1992, Aparecida 2007 — rare, papally convoked events, distinct from CELAM's own periodic General Assemblies (the 40th was held in 2025).[1] [5]
Current leadership
President Cardinal Jaime Spengler OFM (Porto Alegre), elected 2023 for 2023–2027; secretary general Lizardo Estrada Herrera OSA.[19]
Relationship to the Holy See
Via the Pontifical Commission for Latin America (CAL, created 1958) — a Vatican curial organ distinct from CELAM; conference documents acquire their weight through papal approval.[3] [15]

Key figures

Manuel Larraín Errázuriz

1900–1966 · Chile

Bishop of Talca; with Hélder Câmara the founding promoter of CELAM; president until his death in a 1966 car accident — sources differ on whether his term began in 1963, 1964 or 1965.[17] [15]

Hélder Câmara

1909–1999 · Brazil

Co-instigator of CELAM's creation at Rio 1955 and CNBB secretary; never CELAM president. His pastoral trajectory is documented in the tl project — here, the institutional role only.[16]

Jaime de Barros Câmara

1894–1971 · Brazil

Cardinal-archbishop of Rio de Janeiro; first president (1956 – c. 1958/59, per DHIAL).[15]

Avelar Brandão Vilela

1912–1986 · Brazil

President 1966–1972, elected at Mar del Plata to complete Larraín's term; presided over Medellín alongside Paul VI.[17]

Eduardo Pironio

1920–1998 · Argentina

Secretary general 1968–1972 (the Medellín secretariat) and president 1972–1975, then called to the Roman Curia; beatified in 2023.[15]

Aloísio Lorscheider

1924–2007 · Brazil

President 1975–1979; presided at Puebla.[17]

Alfonso López Trujillo

1935–2008 · Colombia

Secretary general 1972–1979 (elected at Sucre) and president 1979–1983 — the pivotal figure of CELAM's reorientation; the interpretation of that reorientation is disputed and documented in the tl project.[18] [17]

Antonio Quarracino

1923–1998 · Argentina

Disambiguation & nuance

The institutional vocabulary invites conflations; these cards keep it straight.

CELAM ≠ CNBB

The CNBB is Brazil's national conference — a CELAM member. The confusion is natural: both were shaped by Hélder Câmara in the 1950s.[16]

CELAM ≠ CAL ≠ Synod for America

CAL is a Vatican curial commission (1958); the Synod for America was a papal Special Assembly (1997). Neither is CELAM.[3]

General Conference ≠ General Assembly

The five General Conferences (Rio, Medellín, Puebla, Santo Domingo, Aparecida) are convoked and opened by the pope, their documents weighted by papal approval. General Assemblies are CELAM's own periodic governance meetings, numbered separately — the 40th met in 2025.[5] [1]

Coordinative, not magisterial

Per its statutes CELAM is an organ of 'contact, communion and service' among the conferences — a characterization attributed to the statutes; it teaches through the conferences and papal approval, not as a magisterium of its own.[15]

The interpretation wars live next door

What Medellín and Puebla meant — liberation theology, the López Trujillo reorientation — is the contested terrain of the sibling tl project. This site records the institutional record: dates, elections, structures, documents.[18]
